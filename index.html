🧠 Strategy Card · PMFG / Peripheral Trading
Peripheral Trading with Planar Maximally Filtered Graphs
One of my favorite recent quant builds: a graph-based equity strategy inspired by Pozzi, Di Matteo, and Aste (2013) that uses a PMFG network to hunt for peripheral names and turn them into a tradeable basket. It lets me express a view about market topology, not just factor exposure.
Graph-based alphaPMFGNetwork filteringPythonBacktest framing

I built this to turn a paper idea into something tradeable and measurable. The workflow takes the daily PMFG selection, enters at the next trading day close, exits at the next rebalance close, and marks the final basket to the latest available close.

What I like most is the interpretability. Instead of running another flat ranking model, I can use network topology as the signal itself and directly test whether peripheral names behave differently from the dense market core.

Inspired by Pozzi, F., Di Matteo, T., & Aste, T. (2013), Exponential smoothing weighted correlations and financial time series modeling.
Signal construction Build a PMFG from the exponentially weighted correlation structure, rank names by the paper-style centrality aggregation, and take the peripheral basket as the tradeable portfolio.
Execution assumption Equal-weight across 10 selected names, entered on the next trading day after the signal and exited on the next signal’s trading day. This keeps the backtest simple and reproducible.
Why I like it The first forward run is negative, which makes the project more real. It forces the framework to earn its keep against live marks instead of surviving on a neat in-sample story.
